My name is **Luke Waldron** and I like to think of myself as a *pragmatic data professional* guided by **sound judgement** and a strong sense of **doing the right thing**, both in my professional work and my personal life.

### How I work

I value **clarity, structure, and intellectual honesty**. I am most comfortable working with analysis that is *explainable and defensible*, even if that means favouring simpler approaches over technically impressive ones that are harder to justify or communicate.

I am comfortable **questioning outcomes**, *surfacing assumptions* and being explicit about **uncertainty or missing data**. I see this not as a weakness in analysis, but as a necessary part of responsible, trustworthy decision‑making.

I enjoy producing **well‑structured, repeatable analytical work**, I am less drawn to unnecessary complexity or vague conclusions that cannot be clearly linked back to real decisions or outcomes.

### My story

My route into data science was **not a traditional academic one**. It began in a customer complaints environment, working with *messy, incomplete and often ambiguous data* where conclusions mattered and fairness was not abstract. Over time, I became less interested in surface‑level reporting and more focused on **why patterns arise**, **how data steers decisions**, and where *unchecked assumptions* can lead to incomplete or unjust outcomes.

This experience led me to formalise my skills through a **Data Science degree ** to be completed alongside my day to day role. The programme has strengthened my *technical foundations*, but just as importantly, it has given me frameworks to **challenge my own thinking**. My goal is not to be a purely theoretical data scientist, but a **reflective practitioner** who combines analysis, context, and judgement to produce insight that genuinely improves decisions.

### Interests and Motivation

My academic interests sit at the **intersection of data science, judgement, and decision‑making**. These interests are grounded in professional experience with *regulated outcomes*, *customer fairness*, and *complaint resolution*, where analytical choices have real and sometimes lasting consequences. I am particularly interested in how *bias, framing, and methodological decisions* influence conclusions, often as much as the models themselves.

Outside of study and work, I am a **family‑oriented person**. I live with my wife, **Charlotte**, and our two children, **Oscar (8)** and **Ivy (6)**. Much of my motivation comes from building a career that is *intellectually rewarding* while remaining grounded in **balance and sustainability**. We enjoy spending time together on holidays, watching films at the weekend and getting outdoors whenever we can. This balance matters to me and informs how I think about sustainable ways of working, learning and progressing over the long term.
