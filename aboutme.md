My name is Luke Waldron and I like to think of myself as a pragmatic data professional who uses excellent judgement and a great sense of doing the right thing in both my professional and personal life.

### How I work

I value clarity, structure, and intellectual honesty. I prefer analysis that is explainable and defensible over approaches that optimise technical performance at the expense of understanding. 

I am comfortable questioning results, surfacing assumptions, and being explicit about limitations or missing data rather than smoothing over uncertainty.

I enjoy building repeatable, well‑structured analytical work. This includes clean notebooks, reusable templates, and processes that others can follow and critique. I dislike unnecessary complexity, vague conclusions, and analysis that cannot be clearly justified or connected to decision‑making.


### My story

My route into data science was not a traditional academic one. It began through practical exposure to messy, incomplete, and often ambiguous data in a customer complaints environment. Over time, I became less interested in surface‑level reporting and more focused on understanding why patterns exist, how decisions are influenced by data, and where poor assumptions can lead to unfair or incomplete outcomes.
This led me to formalise my skills through a Data Science MSc. The programme has strengthened my technical foundations, but just as importantly, it has given me tools to challenge my own thinking. My aim is not to be a purely theoretical data scientist, but a reflective practitioner who combines analysis, context, and judgement to produce insight that genuinely improves decisions.

### Interests and Motivation

My academic interests sit at the intersection of data science, judgement, and decision‑making. This is shaped by my professional experience working with regulated outcomes, customer fairness, and complaint resolution, where analytical choices can have real consequences. I am particularly interested in how bias, framing, and methodological assumptions influence conclusions, often more than the models themselves.

Outside of study and work, I am a family‑oriented person. I live with my wife, Charlotte, and our two children, Oscar (8) and Ivy 96). Much of my motivation comes from wanting to build a career that is both intellectually rewarding and grounded in balance and stability to prpovide for and spend time with the most important people in my world. We enjoy holidays together, spending weekends watching films and rubbish TV and getting outdoors whenever we can. This balance is important to me and shapes how I think about sustainable ways of working and learning.
