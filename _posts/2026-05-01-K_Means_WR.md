# K Means Clustering for NFL Archetypes

### Background

Every April, teams in the National Football League (NFL) draft student athletes from colleges across America to bolster their squads and improve their chances of winning the ultimate prize in their sport; the Lombardi trophy.

The NFL, like other professional sport leagues in America, utilises a draft to allocate athletes fairly to franchises in their sport. This ensures a fair distribution of talent across teams (Sheposh, 2025) and as such the act of grading and analysing athletes and using data to predict sport teams success has grown exponentially through the years (Lopez, 2018). 

It is essential for NFL teams to evaluate talent effectively and efficiently to maximise the chance of **_hitting_** on a draft pick.

An essential part of the draft process is the Scouting Combine, where student athletes gather to be medically examined, measured and evaluated across standardised tests (Kuzmits, 2008). These tests are used in conjunction with a review of collegiate performance and personality assessment to inform the draft status of an athlete. (McGee, 2003)

A good performance at the scouting combine can elevate an athlete’s position in the draft and therefore contract value when entering the league. It is important for athletes to perform well in drills and important for teams to understand how performance in tests equate to performance on the field (Teramoto, 2016).

Similar projects have examined the relationship between combine results and career performance for individuals. Kuzmits found limited correlation between combine and career stats for Quarterbacks (QB’s), Running Backs (RB’s) and Wide Receivers (WR’s) between 1999 and 2004 and Teramoto found limited value in RB’s and WR’s when looking between 2000 and 2009.

This project looks to go further by analysing a 10 year period (2015 to 2025) to determine whether measurables assessed in the scouting combine can be used to group players together into archetypes and whether these archetypes have an increased chance to be successful during their careers. It will then attribute those archetypes to the 2026 combine athletes to support the classification and comparison of those drafted in April 2026.

The project utilises combine performance data such as 40 yard dash time, height, vertical jump height and weight measurements (www.pro-football-reference.com/draft) to cluster archetypes together and then Draft position (https://nextgenstats.nfl.com) to determine; **Can you group athletes together in a meaningful way that outlines their draft position and status?**

#### Height Data Transformation

These needed to be standardised by converting everything to a single metric unit, done through a split of field and then a calculation to give a inches field. ### Data Analytics

Machine Learning can normally be defined through two main approaches; Supervised and Unsupervised Learning. (Sah, 2020) This project uses clustering because the labels for athlete Archetypes are not known in advance. The aim is to generate new labels that can later be used in our classification task for 2026 Athletes.


```python
#define features
features = [
    "Wt",
    "40yd",
    "Vertical",
    "Shuttle",
    "Ht_inches",
]

X = df_final_wr[features].dropna()

```

K‑means is selected because it produces strict, non‑overlapping clusters based on feature similarity, which aligns with the goal of assigning each athlete to only one clear athletic profile.  This relies on Euclidean distance, which supports efficient optimisation in iterative updates of the cluster centroids. (Ikotun et al., 2023)


```python


scaler = StandardScaler()
X_scaled = scaler.fit_transform(X)

```

The data needs to be normalised to ensure features with larger numerical ranges, such as Height (n between 67 and 78 inches), do not dominate features with smaller ranges, such as 40‑yard times (n 4.2 to 5.1 seconds). Normalisation ensures the model identifies meaningful athletic archetypes rather than simply grouping by magnitude.


```python
from sklearn.cluster import KMeans
kmeans = KMeans(
    n_clusters=3,
    random_state=42,
    n_init=10
)

clusters = kmeans.fit_predict(X_scaled)

```

The algorithm begins with randomly selected centroids and assigns each data point to its nearest one. It then recalculates the centroids as the mean of the points in each cluster and iterates until the sum of squared errors (SSE) is minimised. The final cluster assignments form the archetypes used in the project.


```python

df_clustered = df_final_wr.loc[X.index].copy()
```

##### Cluster Definitions

Cluster 0
WR Archetype - Slot/Possession WR
Traits - Lighter and shorter profile with below average speed and explosion.
Expectations - Wins more through route craft and role usage than athletic traits.

Cluster 1
WR Archetype - Outside WR
Traits - Big and tall with strong vertical explosion and good speed and agility for size.
Expectations - Typical boundary receiver profile with capability to win at all three levels.

Cluster 2
WR Archetype - Explosive Separator 
Traits - Smaller, lighter build with the best speed and strong explosion.
Expectations - Suited to pre snap motion and separation rather than a contested catch role.

Cluster 3 
WR Archetype - Big-bodied boundary WR
Traits - Tall and heavy with weaker speed, agility and explosion.
Expectations - Relies more on size and physical presence than separation.


The clustering suggests that wide receivers tend to group into a small number of distinct, physical profiles rather than a single athletic quality, broadly separating players by size, speed, explosion and movement characteristics in ways that align with different offensive roles. These clusters describe tendencies rather than outcomes and assume that combine athletic traits translate reasonably well into the NFL, which is also influenced by offensive scheme, technical skill, decision making, injury luck and development. 

As a result, the archetypes should be treated as probabilistic role indicators rather than evaluations of talent or predictors of success.

The key assumption here is that measurements and traits are a reasonable proxy for how athletes are considered in the draft process. This is evidenced by how certain archetypes are more likely to be drafted and then drafted earlier than others.

This project should be used in conjunction with performance in College, scheme fit as well as technical skills and individual drivers rather than a predictor of NFL success.

#### Improvements and Next Steps

When reflecting on the project, there are improvements and future iterative enhancements that could be made.

Bringing in NFL career statistics could both support the models clustering activity as well as allow us to predict the success of each archetype at the next level. Expanding out to other positions could also be useful as well as bringing in data from pro day workouts instead of just structured combine activty. 

One of the benefits of K means clustering is that it can be run multiple times as centroids are random so running numerous versions could lead to more stability in clustering and reduce sensitivity to centroid placement. There are other clustering activties that could be done and then compared to this model with silhouette score and although DBSCAn or Heirarchical Clustering could have been considered the requirement for every athlete to be labelled and labelled once meant K emans may have been the most appropriate choice.
